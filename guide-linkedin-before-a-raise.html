Getting your LinkedIn ready before a raise

Investors look up founders before they reply. How to set up your profile and company page so they find the same story.

Contents
  1. Why it matters
  2. Your headline
  3. Your About section
  4. Your experience
  5. Your company page
  6. Your leadership team
  7. Posting

01Why it matters

Early-stage investors back people. Before replying to an introduction, many will look up the founders on LinkedIn. An up-to-date profile that tells the same story as your deck builds confidence before you've met.

02Your headline

Your headline appears everywhere you do on LinkedIn. Use it to say what your company does, not just your job title. "CEO at [Company], developing [approach] for [condition]" tells an investor far more than "CEO".

03Your About section

Write three short paragraphs: the problem your company is solving, why you are the person to solve it, and what the company is doing now. Use the same language as your investor narrative.

04Your experience

Make sure each role is current and describes what you did, not just where you were. Highlight the experience most relevant to the company you are building now.

05Your company page

  • Logo and banner that match your brand
  • A description that matches your website's home page
  • Website link and location
  • Recent posts on milestones and news

06Your leadership team

Investors may look up your CSO and other leaders too. Ask them to update their headlines and About sections so the company is described the same way across the team.

07Posting

You don't need to post every day. A steady rhythm of useful updates, such as milestones, publications, conference appearances and hires, shows a company that is moving.

  • Founder headline describes the company
  • About section uses your narrative's language
  • Company page is complete and current
  • Leadership profiles are consistent
