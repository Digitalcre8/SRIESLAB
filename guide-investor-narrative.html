How to write your investor narrative

A step-by-step guide to turning your science into a story investors can follow, remember and repeat to their partners.

Contents

  2. Name the problem
  3. State your insight
  4. Show the evidence
  5. Explain why now
  6. Connect the money to milestones
  7. Introduce the team
  8. Test it out loud

01Before you start

Your investor narrative is a short written document, usually one to two pages, that explains what your company does, why it matters and why it's worth investing in now. Every other piece of material, from your deck to your website, should be built from it.

Write it before you open a slide template. It's much quicker to rework a story in a document than in a designed deck. Get your founder and CSO together for this. You'll need both the science and the business view.

02Name the problem

Start with the patients, the condition and what is failing them today. Write it in words a generalist investor would use.

  • Who is affected, and how many people?
  • What do current treatments or tools get wrong?
  • What does that cost patients, health systems or industry?

Aim for three or four sentences. If you need a paragraph of background before the problem makes sense, the framing is too technical.

03State your insight

What do you know, or what can you do, that others can't? This is the heart of the story. Describe it as an insight, not a method. "We can reach a target others have found undruggable" is an insight. A description of your screening platform is a method.

Try to get it into one sentence. Then write two or three more sentences on the science that makes it credible.

04Show the evidence

List the data that supports your insight, and for each piece write down which investor question it answers. Most map onto a short list:

  • Does it work, and how confident are you?
  • Is it different from what else is in development?
  • How large is the opportunity if it succeeds?

Anything that doesn't answer one of these questions belongs in a technical appendix, not the main story.

05Explain why now

Investors want to know why this is the right moment. It might be new data, a change in regulation, a shift in the market, a partnership, or a technology that only recently became possible. Be specific.

06Connect the money to milestones

Say how much you're raising and what it gets you to. The strongest raises take the company to a milestone that makes it worth materially more, such as a proof-of-concept result, a regulatory step or a first-in-human study.

07Introduce the team

Explain why your team is the right one to deliver this. Focus on relevant experience: the science, development, commercial or company-building track record that makes the plan credible.

08Test it out loud

Read the narrative to someone outside your field, then ask them to explain the company back to you. Whatever they get wrong or leave out is what to rewrite. Repeat until they can tell it back accurately.

  • The problem is clear without background reading
  • The insight fits in one sentence
  • Each piece of evidence answers an investor question
  • The raise is tied to a milestone
  • An outsider can repeat the story
