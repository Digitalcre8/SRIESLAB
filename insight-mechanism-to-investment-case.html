From mechanism of action to investment case

Investors don't fund mechanisms. They fund outcomes, markets and teams. How to translate one into the other without losing the science.

Your mechanism of action is the reason your company exists. To an investor, though, it is a means to an end. The story they need connects that mechanism to patients, to value and to a credible path forward.

Start with the consequence, not the pathway

Describe what changes for a patient if your approach works. Then step back to explain how the mechanism makes that possible. Founders who reverse this order often lose the room before they reach the point.

Translate each scientific claim into an investor question

For every key piece of data, ask what question it answers for an investor. Most map onto a short list:

  • Does it work, and how confident are we?
  • Is it differentiated from what else is coming?
  • How big is the opportunity if it succeeds?
  • What are the next milestones, and what do they cost?
  • Is this the team to get there?

Data that doesn't answer one of these questions probably belongs in the appendix or the data room, not the main story.

Use one visual, built for this audience

A mechanism figure drawn for a journal is rarely right for a pitch. A clear, simplified illustration that shows the before and after of your intervention will do more work than the most accurate pathway diagram.

Agree the story before the slides

Write the narrative down and get your founder and CSO to agree it before anyone opens a slide template. It is far easier to fix a story in a document than in a finished deck.
