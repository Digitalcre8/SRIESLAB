GuidePitch decks · 10 min read

Life science pitch deck structure, slide by slide

A 15-slide outline for an early-stage life science deck, with what each slide needs to do.

Contents
  1. Why 15 slides
  2. The outline
  3. Write the headlines first
  4. One idea per slide
  5. Design for the science
  6. Build a one-page summary
  7. Final checks

01Why 15 slides

Fifteen slides is enough to tell the whole story and short enough to present in a first meeting. Supporting detail goes into an appendix and your data room, where investors can find it when they need it.

The order matters. Each slide should answer a question the investor is already asking because of the slide before.

02The outline

1TitleCompany name and a one-line description of what you do.
2ProblemWho is affected and what is failing them today.
3InsightWhat you know or can do that others can't.
4SolutionYour product or approach, and what changes for patients.
5How it worksThe science, shown with one clear visual.
6EvidenceThe key data that shows it works.
7DifferentiationWhy your approach is better than alternatives.
8MarketThe size of the opportunity, built from the patient population.
9CompetitionWho else is working on this, and where you stand.
10Business modelHow the company creates value: partnering, licensing or building.
11IPWhat is protected and for how long.
12Development planThe route to your next major milestones, with timing.
13TeamFounders, key hires, board and advisers.
14The raiseHow much, what it pays for, and the milestone it reaches.
15SummaryThe three things you want the investor to remember.

03Write the headlines first

Give every slide a headline that is a full sentence stating its point, such as "Our lead compound halved tumour growth in two models", not "Preclinical data". Read the headlines in order. If they tell the story on their own, the structure works.

04One idea per slide

If a slide makes two points, split it or move one to the appendix. Busy slides make investors read instead of listen.

05Design for the science

A mechanism figure drawn for a journal rarely works in a pitch. Use one simplified visual that shows the before and after of your intervention. Make charts show the single comparison that matters, with the result labelled directly.

06Build a one-page summary

Many investors ask for a one-pager before they take a meeting. Write it from the same story: problem, insight, evidence, market, team, raise. Keep it to one side of A4.

07Final checks

  • Headlines alone tell the story
  • Every slide makes one point
  • The science has one clear visual
  • The raise is linked to a milestone
  • Deck, one-pager and website use the same words
