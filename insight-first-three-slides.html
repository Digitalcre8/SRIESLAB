Your first three slides decide the meeting

Most investors decide within a few minutes whether to keep listening. Here is how to use the opening of your deck so they do.

Investors see a lot of decks. The opening slides are where they decide whether yours is worth their full attention, so those slides carry more weight than any others.

Life science founders often open with the science, because the science is what they know best and what they are proudest of. The trouble is that an investor can't judge the science until they know why it matters. Without that frame, even excellent data reads as detail.

Slide one: the problem, in plain words

Name the patients, the condition and what is failing them today. Use the language a generalist investor would use, not the language of your last grant application. If a partner at the fund needs to repeat it to their colleagues on Monday, could they?

Slide two: why your approach is different

This is where the science comes in, at the level of insight rather than method. What do you know or can you do that others can't? One clear sentence here beats a dense mechanism diagram.

Slide three: why now, and what this round buys

Show what has changed to make this the right moment, then connect the money to a milestone. Investors want to see that the round takes you to a point where the company is worth materially more.

A quick test

Show your first three slides to someone outside your field for two minutes, then ask them to explain the company back to you. Whatever they get wrong is what to rewrite first.

Everything else is evidence

Once the opening has done its job, the rest of the deck supports it: data, team, competitive landscape, plan and ask. Each slide should answer a question the investor is already asking because of how you opened.
